When I was a kid I was addicted to National Geographic channel. I could watch it forever. My favourite shows were those that included big cats. I always admired their agility and thought they're beautiful. My favourite cat was the cheetah, the fastest land animal and an admirable creature. I loved it so much that I dressed up as a cheetah every year for Purim (a jewish festivity where we wear costumes). All the kids were changing costumes every year, but I stayed loyal to the cheetah.

I remember one time there was a program about a family of cheetahs. The cheetah mom had three little cubs and she was struggling to feed them all by herself. The cubs were starving and the narrator in the background explained that many cubs die in their youth. I was terrified by the idea that they might starve to death so I prayed that the mother would manage to hunt something for them. Then she finally saw a pack of gazelles. She sneaked slowly towards them and waited for the right moment to make her move. She came closer and closer, and then started running at an amazing speed. I crossed my fingers as I saw the gazelles zigzagging and making huge jumps. "Go faster! You can do it! You're faster than her!" Then she got the gazelle and I felt a huge relief as I saw her young cubs eating, knowing they're not going to starve to death this time.

So there goes my story with cheetahs. But as I told you before, I watched a lot of content on National Geogrpahic and on several occasions there were programms on gazelles. On one of these ocasions a mother gazelle just had a newborn little gazelle. A couple of moments after his birth he was already on his feet! The narrator explained that in nature you have to learn how to run really fast if you want to survive. When I saw a cheetah approaching from a distance I was terrified. The young gazelle had no chance and it was alive for less than one day! I was devastated when I saw the baby gazelle in the cheetahs mouth while the mom was a couple of steps away and couldn't do anything to save her kid. It was heartbreaking.

Now you probably noticed that the two stories are very similar, but are told from a different point of view. They have different "framing". How come I changed sides? One time I was at the cheetah's side and then I changed it to the gazelle's. What's wrong with me? Do I have no values? I'm an absolute hypocrite! I guess most of you would have reacted the same way as I did as I watched these two programms. Nor the cheetah nor the gazelle is evil. They both do what they need to do in order to survive and the fact that we support one side or another has to do more with the story we're told than with the real facts that are quite simple; the cheetah must hunt and kill other animals in order to survive. Such is the power of narrating a specicific point of view and in the world of rhetorics it's called 'framing' due to the way it frames our way of thinking about certain situations. I think that another good way to call it would be lenses, bucause of the way it paints the reality we see.

One story shows a brave young mom that's fighting for her cubs, and the other story shows her as a cold blooded killer. Now this can be applied on almost every story we hear, and the dangerous part is that we're not even aware of the fact that we're hearing a framed story, that we see it through someone else's lenses. It has nothing to do with specific ideology, it happens everywhere and it's part of human society.

So in today's world where we get our information from social media and we tend to follow only the pages that tells us stories that fit our frame, we become anware of the other sides of these stories and there's always another side. It's true that some news are just complete lies and fake, but the majority are not. But the fact that they're not fake doesn't mean that they show the whole picture, they never do. They'll always frame it in a way that suits their interest and that's where we must be more suspicious and try to think how could this story be told from a different point of view. Just think of the difference between a doctor telling his pacient that he has 90% percent to survive or if he tells him that he has 10% to die. Or if we call someone a 'freedom fighter' in contrast to a 'terrorist'. You see, this usage of the language imposes a certain framing in a subtle way that effects the way we afterwards interpret the story.

My goal is not to show you how "the other side" is trying to manipulate his people with twisted messages because that would also be framing and I want to remain impartial. The truth is that EVERYONE is doing it and your goal should be to notice it and try to think how could this story be told from a different point of view, but it requires a lot of critical thinking which few people have. I think that the moment we as a society will become more aware of this phenomenon, we will realize that we're not that different after all and hopefully we'll be able to save many people's lives by preventing wars.
